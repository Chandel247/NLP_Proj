NLP semester project · Amazon ML Challenge 2024

Which Number Is Width

Read a product attribute — weight, volume, voltage, wattage, or one of three dimensions — off the product photograph alone. Half the corpus turns out to be solvable and half turns out to be under-determined by the text entirely: the number is legible, but nothing written on the image says which dimension it is. A 2B vision-language model, fine-tuned to point at the right number, lifts that half from 38.8% to 56.0% on a test split nothing was tuned on, and to 58.8% with the text system as a fallback, taking the whole task from 52.1% to 63.2% — and when its photograph is replaced with a blank, most of that gain disappears.

395,046 labelled rows available 8 attributes 25,000 row working set 24,926 images OCR'd image only — no catalogue text Qwen2-VL-2B + LoRA, one Kaggle T4

01 · The task

Conditional extraction from a photograph

Given

a product image and the name of a wanted attribute, return its value and unit — or decline, rather than guess.

The 2024 schema is image_link, group_id, entity_name, entity_value. There is no title, no bullet points, no description. OCR is the only evidence, which makes the quality of the reader the whole ballgame — and makes the attribute name part of the input, not a fixed target.

03 · Diagnosis

The number is there. Its name is not.

A representative length row, with everything the model is given: 

attribute:  width
OCR text:   5.7cm/2.24in  9.3cm/3.66in  4cm/1.57in  3.5cm/1.37in  12.3cm/4.84in
gold:       9.3 centimetre

The answer is present, which is why the ceiling is 71.7%. But the text contains a bare list of measurements from a size chart. Nothing in it marks one as the width. The information that would disambiguate — which arrow points along which axis — exists only in the image's geometry.

The obvious remedy is a A x B x C parser that assigns by position. It was measured before it was built, and it does not apply: 

length rows: 12,574
  neither                                 12,200   97.0%
  has A x B x C triple                       213    1.7%
  has an explicitly labelled dimension       163    1.3%

gold matches which triple position (rows with a triple):
  depth   pos1: 18%  pos2: 28%  pos3: 55%
  width   pos1: 22%  pos2: 26%  pos3: 52%
  height  pos1: 18%  pos2: 14%  pos3: 68%

97% of length rows carry neither a triple nor a labelled dimension — and where a triple does appear, all three attributes favour position 3, so there is no ordering convention to exploit. Such a parser would reach about 0.9% of the corpus.

The conclusion

For half this corpus, no text model can succeed — however good its OCR. Resolving depth from width requires associating a printed number with the axis it annotates, which is a spatial judgement about the image.

This reverses an earlier decision to demote the vision-language model. That call was made on a five-attribute sample where headroom above the rules was about seven points. On the real task the VLM is not upside — for depth, width and height it is the only remaining lever. Section 05 pulls it.

04 · The system

What is built, and what it cost

64.0%

Gold values recoverable with Apple Vision OCR, against 42.3% for EasyOCR on the identical 175 rows. Free, no GPU, 24 img/s on six workers.

71.9%

Weak-supervision coverage. Programme search runs backwards from the gold value, so it labels the correct span even where five candidates compete.

0.188%

Provably-wrong label floor at natural frequency — six rules, each confirmed against an image. Concentrated in one attribute worth 1.24% of the data.

S6 One score on the untouched test splitevery choice above was made looking at the 406 validation rows · 1,256 test rows, scored once, no tuning · section 06 58.8% test

The tagger's first run failed outright — 34.0% against the rules' 45.8%, abstaining on 56% of rows. It had collapsed to predicting all-O: with ~44 tokens of which two or three carry a span, O is 93% of targets, and under genuine ambiguity the cross-entropy optimum is to predict nothing. Weighting span tags 8× against O, plus a decode fallback that trusts the unit head when no unit span is tagged, moved abstention from 56.0% to 13.8% and the score from 34.0% to 48.4%.

Why it worked: the failure was a habit, not a limit

Zero-shot, the model barely beat chance on rows it could have answered, and its letters did not follow the answers. Where the gold candidate actually sits in OCR order is uneven — mostly first or second — and the untrained model preferred second and third. The fine-tuned model’s letters now track where the answers are.

That diagnosis shaped training. Option order is reshuffled on every example, so a positional habit earns nothing and cannot be learned; only the final letter is supervised; the vision encoder stays frozen and 18.5M adapter weights (0.83% of the model) are trained — one epoch over 6,036 rows at 512×512, 394 minutes on a single T4.

What was checked before believing it

Rescored Every prediction re-scored independently of the notebook: 209 / 406, zero disagreements.
Leakage All 406 rows come from the validation split. No shared filenames, no byte-identical images. Twenty-one perceptual-hash matches were inspected: different products with different gold values. Dropping them moves the score to 51.9%.
Paired Against the cascade on identical rows: LoRA right where the cascade is wrong 110 times, the reverse 34. Exact McNemar p = 1.5×10−10.
Text prior Shuffling removes position, not magnitude — “height is usually the largest number”. The best such rule, fitted on training rows, scores 46.3% on the 2+-candidate rows; LoRA scores 70.8%, and random 35.5%.
The image The one explanation left was a subtler prior in the candidate text. Replacing each photo with a blank takes accuracy from 70.8% to 47.9% — the level of the text-only rule. Tested below.

Depth is mostly a text problem: it is usually the largest number on a size chart, and the blank-image model already gets two thirds of it. Width and height are not — without the photograph the model is near chance on both, and with it gains thirty points each. That is the question in this page’s title, answered by looking. “Photo is worth” is real minus blank; per-attribute rows are 84–87 each, so read them to about ±10 points.

Routing: taking the cascade’s answer only where the model has none

The two systems fail in different places, so the next step combines them. The rule was written down before it was scored, because these 406 rows are the only evaluation set and fitting a router to them would be tuning on the answers: use the fine-tuned model whenever it answers, and fall back to the cascade when OCR gave it no candidate to choose from.

The gain is mechanical. The model is silent on the 94 rows with no length candidate, and the cascade’s own text extraction is right on 14 of them. Nothing is lost, because the router never overrides an answer the model gave.

A smarter router is not worth building. The 20 rows between the router and the oracle are all rows where both systems answered and disagreed — and on those 179 disagreements the model is right 110 times, the cascade 20. In 17 of the 20 the cascade’s answer was already on the model’s list, so “the cascade saw something the model couldn’t” does not separate them. Picking out 20 rows without giving back any of the 110 would need the model’s confidence, and the best case is 4.9 points. The test run saves that confidence for every row, so the question can be revisited without another GPU run.

07 · Corrections

Seven claims this project had to withdraw

Each was stated with confidence, then measured. The measurements are cheap; the confident guesses were not.

01 “The multimodal arm is worth about ten points.” Measured on the earlier corpus: 4.5. A 450-image probe costing one minute predicted a 38-minute full pass to within 1.6 points.
02 “Label noise is 1.93% of the corpus.” That was measured on a sample stratified equally across five attributes, which over-weighted the one broken attribute tenfold. At natural frequency: 0.188%.
03 “The rules already capture 90% of the ceiling, so the lever is the image channel, not the selection model.” True of five attributes; they were half the corpus. On the full task the rules capture 46% of the length ceiling, and the selection problem is the dominant one.
04 “A dimension-triple parser will close the length gap.” 97% of length rows have no triple, and where one exists there is no positional convention. It would reach 0.9% of the corpus. Measured before it was built.
05 “The bar for the VLM is the cascade’s 36.2%.” 36.2% was measured across the whole validation split, not on the VLM’s evaluation rows. Re-run on those exact 406 rows the cascade scores 32.8%, so the true margin is 18.7 points, not 15.3.
06 “If fine-tuning works, the model’s letters will even out toward a quarter each.” Evaluation keeps options in OCR order, where the gold answer is itself uneven — (a) 36.6%, (c) 16.0%. A correct model matches that skew; it does not flatten it. Using the wrong test would have read success as a warning sign.
07 “The 17 rows the cascade finds outside the candidate list are most of the oracle’s headroom.” The oracle adds 34 rows to the fine-tuned model, so 17 is half. Fourteen sit where OCR produced no candidates, and a fixed router recovers them; of the 20 rows still missing, 17 were on the model’s list all along.

08 · Method

What keeps the numbers honest

The working set was rebuilt. A previously used sample of this dataset held 3,808 rows stratified across five attributes. Rebuilt by simple random sample from all 263,859 rows: 25,000 rows, eight attributes, 712 group ids against 166, with natural proportions matching the source within 0.2 points. The rebuild is what exposed the length problem; on the old sample it was invisible.

Splits are grouped, not random. Assigned by group_id and stored in the working-set file, so they cannot drift between training and evaluation. group_id is category-level rather than product-level, so this is a partial leakage guard, not a complete one.

Three metrics, because “correct” is ambiguous. value is magnitude within 1% ignoring units; dim requires the same dimensional family and magnitude in base units; strict demands the identical canonical unit. dim is primary here: gold units are normalised by the challenge to grams and centimetres while packaging prints 12 OZ, so a right answer in another unit is a success that value would score as failure.

Gold is an interval. 4.7% of 2024 labels are genuine ranges ([110.0, 130.0] volt on a universal power supply), so gold is (low, high, unit) throughout, with scalars as the degenerate case.

Models are local and open. No hosted model API appears anywhere in the pipeline. Apple Vision OCR and the tagger run on an M2 Pro; the VLM is fine-tuned on Kaggle’s free T4, where one session is capped at 12 hours — which is why the run is a single epoch at 512×512 rather than two at 768×768, a 25-hour job that could not have finished.

Every figure here is provisional in one respect: the labels are the challenge's own scraped values, and only 13 of the 175 held-out rows have been proved wrong automatically. Hand-verification remains the one step that cannot be automated, and the review sheet is triaged so that the 65 decision-relevant rows come first.
